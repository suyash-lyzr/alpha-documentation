Once an app is live, two workspace tabs show how it behaves in production: **Production Logs** for every line it writes and every request it answers, and **Production Metrics** for its traffic, errors, speed and resource use. This page also covers the **Published** page and what to do when a version fails. To republish, roll back or change settings, see [Manage a published app](/publish/manage).

Both tabs read from the live app, not from the session's machine, so they work while the machine sleeps. Only the session's owner sees them, and they open only when you pick them from **+**: the agent reading production never opens them for you.

<Note>
The production tabs appear in the **+** menu once one of the session's apps has gone live and has an address. If you don't see **Publish** at all, publishing isn't turned on for your Alpha.
</Note>

## Read your app's logs

Open **+** → **Production Logs**. Newest lines sit at the bottom, like a terminal. The tab loads new lines by itself, or right away from the refresh button. It opens on the app you published most recently; if the session has more than one, switch with the app menu at the left of the filter row.

| Control | Options | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Search the lines…** | Up to 200 characters | Keeps lines that contain the text. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or click away to apply it. |
| Time range | **Last hour**, **Last 24 hours**, **Last 7 days** | Starts on **Last hour**. Longer ranges add the date to each line. |
| Level | **All levels**, **Errors**, **Warnings+**, **Info+** | Shows lines at that level and above. **Errors** includes critical lines. |
| Kind | **All lines**, **Requests**, **App output**, **System** | **App output** is what your app prints. **Requests** has one line per request, with method, path, status and time in ms. **System** is what Alpha's hosting reports about the app, such as a crash or a memory limit. |
| **↑ Older lines…** | 200 lines a page | Loads earlier lines. The tab keeps the newest 2,000. **Latest** jumps back to the bottom. |

Log times are in UTC; hover one for the full timestamp. A new time range, level or search reads the log again from the start, while the kind only filters lines already loaded.

## Hand a log line to the agent

Hover any line and a speech-bubble icon appears. It copies the line into the composer, so you can ask the agent about it without retyping.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hover the line that explains the problem">
    The icon appears at the right end of the row. Its tooltip reads "Paste this line into the chat, quoted as the app's output, for the agent to look at".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the icon">
    Alpha adds the line after anything you've already typed, with the app, time, version and level, and terminal colour codes stripped. It is quoted as the app's output, so the agent treats it as data, not instructions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add context and send">
    Nothing is sent until you press send. The pasted text ends with "What went wrong here? Fix it and redeploy.", so sending it as it is asks the agent to fix the cause and republish, and it reads the new version's logs before it reports done. To have the cause explained without a republish, change that last line before you send.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also describe the symptom in your own words. When you say a live app is broken or slow, the agent reads production's logs and metrics before it changes any code, and quotes the line that explains the problem. Its reads don't open the production tabs: to look along with it, open **Production Logs** or **Production Metrics** from **+**. Once the fix is made, the agent says a republish would ship it and waits for you to ask. After you do, it reads the new version's logs once more before it reports done.

## Check traffic, errors and resources

Open **+** → **Production Metrics**. The tab, titled **Monitoring**, has two cards, and each card has its own period: **Past one hour**, **Past 24 hours** or **Past one week**. **Performance** starts on the past week and **Resources** on the past hour.

<Frame caption="Performance answers whether the app is up and fast. Resources answers whether it's running out of room. Illustrative values.">
  <img src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/suyash-lyzr/alpha-documentation@0b61fd2f913704672c92bd329ca2bad683e14924/images/publish/prod-metrics-tab.webp" alt="Two cards. PERFORMANCE, set to Past one week, has a Requests chart of daily traffic peaks from Nov 8 to Nov 15 with Connected and Failed lines, then HTTP Statuses (2xx, 3xx, 4xx, 5xx) and Request Durations in ms (p50, p95, p99, with occasional p99 spikes). RESOURCES, set to Past one hour, shows CPU Utilization around 25% and Memory Utilization around 40% of the limit." />
</Frame>

| Chart | Lines | Read it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Requests** | Connected, Failed | Failed requests are the ones your app answered with a server error (5xx). |
| **HTTP Statuses** | 2xx, 3xx, 4xx, 5xx | A rise in 4xx means the app refused requests, such as not found. A rise in 5xx means the app failed inside. |
| **Request Durations (ms)** | p50, p95, p99 | A low p50 with a high p99 means most requests are fast but a few are slow. That's often the first request after a quiet spell, or one slow route. |
| **CPU Utilization** | CPU | The busiest instance's share of the CPU it's allowed. |
| **Memory Utilization** | Memory | The busiest instance's share of its {memory} memory. Near 100% means the next spike can crash the instance. |

Hover a chart to see each line's value at that moment, in your time zone. Each chart title has an ⓘ that explains the chart in plain words. Request counts draw a flat line at zero when the app is quiet. A duration, CPU or memory chart with nothing to plot says "No Data Available".

<Note>
Metrics arrive one to three minutes behind, so the last few minutes of traffic may not show yet. The tab updates by itself.
</Note>

## See everything you've published

The **Published** row in the sidebar, with a badge counting your apps, opens a page of every app and API published from your sessions: "Every app and API published from your sessions, and the address each one answers at." Use it to find an address, get back to the session an app came from, or clear out apps you no longer need.

<Frame caption="One card per app, newest publish first, two to a row on a wide screen. A failed card shows its last error in red.">
  <img src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/suyash-lyzr/alpha-documentation@0b61fd2f913704672c92bd329ca2bad683e14924/images/publish/published-page.webp" alt="The Published page with 3 SERVICES, a search box Search by name, address or session, the filters All, Live, Publishing, Failed and Any time, and the count 3 SERVICES · 2 LIVE. The harbor-api card (API, from Harbor booking agent) reads Not published — no address with the red error The app never listened on 0.0.0.0:8080 within 4 minutes and failed · published 41 min ago. The harbor-bookings card (App, from Harbor bookings · 3 versions · database) shows harbor-bookings.apps.example.com and live · published 2 h ago with View session and Open app. The harbor-triage card (API, 2 versions) shows harbor-triage.apps.example.com and live · published 1 d ago." />
</Frame>

Above the cards, narrow the list three ways. A count line under them reads, for example, "4 services · 3 live", or "2 of 4 services · 3 live" while a filter hides some.

| Control | Options | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Search by name, address or session** | Free text | Keeps apps whose name, address or session title contains the text. |
| State | **All**, **Live**, **Publishing**, **Failed** | **Publishing** is any app with a rollout under way. |
| Published within | **Any time**, **Today**, **Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days** | Filters by the app's last publish, counting calendar days with today included, as the sidebar does. |

When nothing matches, the page reads "Nothing matches." Each card shows one app:

| Part of a card | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Mark, name, tag | The mark of the app's framework or language, its name, and a tag reading **App** or **API** (an agent's API, called from code). |
| from *session* | The session it was published from, then its folder when it isn't the workspace root, its number of versions once there's more than one, and "database" if it has a production database. |
| Address | Opens the app in a new tab, with a copy button (tooltip "Copy link"). It's [your own domain](/publish/custom-domains) once that's **Live**, else the address on your Alpha's domain where it has one. Reads "No address yet" until a version goes live, or "Not published — no address" for a failed app that never had one. |
| Last error | On a failed app, the error of its last publish, in red. Hover it for the whole line. |
| Footer | The state (**live**, **failed**, or the step a publish is on, such as **building**) and "published 2 h ago". Then **View session**, which opens the session; **Open app**, once it has an address; and a trash button. |

The trash button (tooltip "Delete service and its database", or "Delete service") is off while the app is publishing. It turns the footer into "Delete harbor and its database?" with **Keep** and **Delete**. Deleting here is the same as **Delete service** on the [Publish tab](/publish/manage#delete-a-service): the app and its production database go for good.

The page lists only your own sessions. Until you publish something, it reads "Nothing is published yet" and "Open a session and ask the agent to deploy its app; it shows up here, with the address it answers at." To republish, roll back or edit settings, use the session's **Publish** tab.

## When a publish fails

A failed version never takes traffic. If an earlier version was serving, it keeps serving, so your live app doesn't go down. The **Publish** tab marks the step that failed and shows the first lines of the error and a hint. Under them, the version's **Log**, open as on every version, has the build's own output, and the session's transcript gets a failure card with the same hint and the end of the log.

A red box reads the build ended with FAILURE and Dependency install failed — make sure the lockfile matches the manifest and every dependency exists. Below it are Version 3 · live and Versions 2 and 1 · ready, each with Roll back." />

The agent reads the same log, so asking it is usually the fastest fix. The hints below are quoted as they appear; [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting#a-publish-fails) collects them with the rest.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="“Couldn't recognize this stack”">
    **Causes:** Alpha couldn't find an app it knows how to publish in that folder. Often the wrong folder was published.

    **Fix:** Ask the agent to publish the folder that holds the app.

    ```text
    The publish couldn't recognize the stack. Check which directory holds the app, then publish that one.
    ```
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“No start command was found”">
    **Causes:** Alpha couldn't tell how to start the app in production.

    **Fix:** Ask the agent to set how the app starts, or set **Run command** in **Edit settings** on the [Publish tab](/publish/manage#change-how-the-app-runs), then republish.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“Dependency install failed”">
    **Causes:** The lockfile is out of step with the manifest, or a dependency doesn't exist under that name or version.

    **Fix:** Regenerate the lockfile in the session, then republish.

    ```text
    The publish failed installing dependencies. Bring the lockfile in step with the manifest, check it builds, and republish.
    ```
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“A build command failed” or “The build exceeded …”">
    **Causes:** The app's build step fails, often because it needs a build-time variable such as `VITE_*` or `NEXT_PUBLIC_*` that wasn't picked for production. Or the build takes longer than {buildMax}.

    **Fix:** Find the failing command in the **Log**. Pick missing variables in **Edit settings** → **Environment**; only variables picked there reach production. For a slow build, drop steps production doesn't need.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“The app never listened on 0.0.0.0:PORT within …”">
    **Causes:** The app didn't start serving within {listenMax}. It may be bound to `localhost` or a fixed port instead of the `PORT` it's given. It may be running a development server. Or the app needs its database tables at start, and production's database is still empty.

    **Fix:** Bind `0.0.0.0` and read `PORT`. If the app needs tables at start, ask the agent to have the app create them as it starts, or push the session's schema. See [Production database and sign-in](/publish/production-data).

    ```text
    The publish failed because the app never listened on its port. Make it bind 0.0.0.0 on $PORT, start it as a production app, and republish.
    ```
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“Out of memory at …”">
    **Causes:** The app used more than its {memory} limit. Watch **Memory Utilization** on the **Production Metrics** tab.

    **Fix:** Hold less in memory, such as large files read whole or big in-process caches. The limit is set for your whole Alpha; ask your administrator if the app needs more.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“The attached secret set needs … — nothing was deployed”">
    **Causes:** A [secret set](/secrets#secret-sets) is attached to the session, or to the setup it started from, and every name in it goes to production. At least one of those names has no value of yours: not in the set, not as a secret of the session, and not among your global secrets. The version fails while planning, before anything is built, with the hint "add them on the Secrets page, then deploy again".

    **Fix:** Open the **Secrets** page, fill in your value for each name the message lists, then publish again. A set someone shared with you through a setup needs your own values too.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“Your organization policy forbids public services”">
    **Causes:** Your organization's settings don't allow apps that anyone can open, and published apps are public.

    **Fix:** This is a setting on your Alpha's side, not in your code. Pass the message to your administrator.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

A version that fails with "the deployment was stopped" isn't an error: someone clicked [**Stop**](/publish/publish-your-app#stop-a-rollout) while it was building. Republish when you're ready.

A hint about permissions or credentials is a setup problem on your Alpha's side that your code can't fix. Pass it to your administrator. For limits such as source size, see [Limits](/reference/limits).

## The service is up, but refuses its own address

Some frameworks only answer the web addresses on an allow-list. If your app's list leaves out its published address, every visitor gets an error page. After each rollout, Alpha checks the app at its real address. If the app refuses it, the version still goes live, with a yellow warning that starts "The service is up, but".

The check uses the app's first address, so an app that refuses [your own domain](/publish/custom-domains) or a renamed address gets no warning: open the app there to check it.

When the agent publishes, it sees this warning too, and it fixes the app's settings and republishes before it reports done. If you manage the allow-list yourself, ask the agent to add the published address to it, keeping the Preview's address allowed, and republish:

```text
The published app refuses its own address. Allow its published address in the framework's allowed hosts, keep the Preview and localhost allowed, and republish.
```
