A published app is a service with its own page in the session's **Publish** tab, where you republish it, change how it runs, switch between its versions and delete it. To put an app online the first time, see [Publish your app](/publish/publish-your-app). For its logs and metrics, see [Monitor and troubleshoot production](/publish/monitor).

Pick the action by what you want to change:

| You want to | Use | Rebuilds the app? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ship the latest code | **Republish** | Yes, a new version |
| Change the port, run command, environment variables, database or sign-in | **Edit settings** | Yes, a new version |
| Call off a rollout that's still building | **Stop** | No. The version serving stays as it is. |
| Undo a bad release, or serve a newer version again | **⋯** → **Roll back**, or **Roll back** / **Roll forward** on a version in **History** | No. It only switches which version is live. |
| Take the app offline for good | **⋯** → **Delete service** | No, and it can't be undone. |

<Note>
If you don't see the **Publish** pill or the **Publish** tab, publishing isn't turned on for your Alpha. Ask your administrator.
</Note>

## Read a service's page

Open **+** → **Publish** and pick the service. On a wide workspace the page has two columns: the service on the left, from its actions down to its history, and a **Details** rail on the right. On a narrow one, **Details** follows the history.

| Part | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Header | The name, which opens the switcher (see below); an **App** or **API** tag when the session has an agent's API; the state (**live**, **failed**, or the step a rollout is on, such as **building**). Then **Edit settings**, **Republish**, **Stop** while a rollout can still be stopped, and **⋯** (**More actions**), which holds **Roll back** and **Delete service**. |
| Address line | The address, which opens the app, a copy button (tooltip "Copy address") and **Open**. "A new version is rolling out." shows under it during a rollout, and "also …" with the app's second address when it has one. Before a first version is live it reads "Your app's address appears here once it is live." |
| **Production database** | Only when the service has one: when it was made, whether a schema arrived, and the push from the session. See [Production database and sign-in](/publish/production-data). |
| **History** | Every version, newest first, on a timeline: "Version N", its state, and when it ran, who started it ("by the agent" or "from the dialog") and how long it took. The open version unfolds with its steps while it rolls out or after it fails, any error or hint, and its **Log**. |
| **Details** | How the open version runs: its stack, how it starts, its port and where its code came from. Then **Environment**, which lists variable names, never their values, and **Services**, which says what **Database** and **Sign-in** production gets. |

The **Log** under the open version is open from the start, on every version. It shows the build's own output with **follow**, a copy button and **Hide log** / **Show log**; see [Watch the rollout](/publish/publish-your-app#watch-the-rollout). Secret values appear as `[redacted]`.

Under **Environment**, names you picked show first. Names Alpha sets for the app's database or sign-in follow, paler. A variable of the same name that you pick takes their place.

### Switch between a session's apps

A session can publish up to [{publishPerSession}](/reference/limits), one per directory, such as a web app and its API. Click the name in the header to open the switcher: every service of the session with its address and state, then **Publish another app**, which sends the agent "Deploy this."

When one of them is an [AI agent's API](/agents/build-an-agent), each service carries a tag: **App** for "The app people open", **API** for "The agent's API: called from code, not opened in a browser". An API's address line has **Copy endpoint** in place of the copy button and **Open**, and a note under it: "The agent's API, for calling it from code. The app people open is harbor." Click the app's name to open its page.

## Republish your changes

A republish sends "Republish harbor." to the agent, with the service's name, as if you'd typed it. The agent publishes the session's latest code as a new version of the same service, and the **Publish** tab follows the rollout. There are two places to start one:

| Where | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Republish** in the service's header | Sends the message at once. Its tooltip reads "Sends “Republish harbor.” to the agent — it publishes the latest changes as a new version". |
| The **Republish** pill beside **Share** | Opens a popover first (tooltip "Republish, or copy its address"). The pill shows once a service is live, and stands for the service the **Publish** tab shows, or else the first live one. |

The popover is headed **Republish** and has three rows: **Status**, such as "Live · published 2 h ago"; **Address**, which opens the app in a new tab, with a copy button; and **Access**, "Public · anyone with the URL". Under them, a line reads "The agent publishes the latest changes as a new version; the Publish tab follows the rollout." **Open Publish tab** opens the tab, and **Republish** sends the message. The link icon beside the pill copies the address without opening anything.

<Frame caption="The Republish popover. The address can be read and copied even while the button is held.">
  <img src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/suyash-lyzr/alpha-documentation@0bce4e6aa460e3426daece54f29c495f4565999a/images/publish/publish-pill-republish.webp" alt="The body of the Republish popover: Status Live · published 2 h ago, Address harbor-bookings.apps.example… with a copy-link button, and Access Public · anyone with the URL. Below them: The agent publishes the latest changes as a new version; the Publish tab follows the rollout. Buttons Open Publish tab and Republish." />

Asking in your own words works too, such as "Republish harbor with the latest changes." Saying that the live app needs a fix isn't a request to republish: the agent makes the fix, says a republish would ship it, and waits for you to ask. See [When does the agent publish?](/publish/publish-your-app#when-does-the-agent-publish).

A republish asks you nothing. It keeps the address, port, run command, environment variables and production services, and attaches the same production database. Every name in the session's [secret sets](/secrets#secret-sets) goes with it too. The old version keeps answering until the new one serves.

Both buttons are held while a turn runs ("The agent is busy — ask once it is done"), because a message then would [steer](/chat/queue-and-steer) the agent instead. In the popover, the reason replaces the line above the buttons, and the address can still be opened and copied. During any of the session's rollouts or rollbacks, the header button is held with "A rollout is in progress", and the pill reads **Publishing…** and opens the **Publish** tab.

## Change how the app runs

**Edit settings** opens **Settings for harbor**, a form holding every setting of the next version. You change them by hand and republish from it, without the agent. The form reads your project, so opening it wakes the session's machine if it's asleep.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the form">
    On the service's page, click **Edit settings**. It's off while any of the session's rollouts runs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change what you need">
    The rows sit under **Build and run** and **Production services**. After you change **Run command**, press <kbd>Enter</kbd>, click away or click **Check** so Alpha checks it. **Republish** stays off until it has.

Under Build and run, the Address row says Kept when you republish. beside a read-only field https://harbor-bookings-4k2p.apps.example.com, with also https://harbor-bookings.apps.example.com under it." />

  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the environment">
    Click **change** beside the variable names. Current ones become **kept:** chips, and × drops one. Click a secret's chip to include it, or **Add a variable** to type a `NAME` and `value`. **keep all as they are** undoes your changes.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Republish">
    Click **Republish** in the bar at the bottom. The page then follows the new version, marked "from the dialog".

### What each setting does

| Setting | What it does | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Address** | The public address. | Read-only: the name is the address. For a new one, publish under a new name and delete this service. |
| **Port** | Where the app listens. Alpha sends requests there and sets `PORT`. | 1 to 65535. Chips offer ports seen listening, **page** on one serving a web page. |
| **Run command** | Starts the app in production. Empty uses the command Alpha detected. | One line of at most 500 characters, checked before you republish. Hidden for projects that set how they start themselves. |
| **Environment** | "Only what you pick here reaches production, at build time and at runtime." | Letters, digits and `_`, not starting with a digit, at most 64 characters, each name once. Names Alpha sets for the app, such as `PORT`, and names reserved for the machine's shell, such as `PATH`, are refused. |
| **Database** | Gives production a PostgreSQL database of its own. | Starts empty. Your own `DATABASE_URL` wins. See [Production database and sign-in](/publish/production-data). |
| **Sign-in** | Gives production the session's [app sign-in](/workspace/app-sign-in): the same accounts and **Auth** tab. | Sign-in works on the app's own address. |

Nothing is inherited from the session's machine, so build-time variables such as `VITE_…` or `NEXT_PUBLIC_…` must be picked too. Secrets travel by name, so no value shows in the form or the transcript. When a [session secret and a global secret](/secrets) share a name, production gets the session's value.

One exception to "only what you pick": when a [secret set](/secrets#secret-sets) is attached to the session, or to the setup it started from, every name in it reaches production on every version, without being picked here. Each takes the session's own secret of that name if there is one, else your value in the set, else your global secret of that name. If none has a value, nothing is published, and the version fails with "the attached secret set needs STRIPE_SECRET_KEY — nothing was deployed" and the hint "add them on the Secrets page, then deploy again".

## Roll back or forward

Rolling back puts a version that already exists back in front of visitors. Alpha keeps the versions it built, so nothing is rebuilt and the session's machine isn't needed.

| From | Click | Makes live |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The header's **⋯** menu | **Roll back**, which names the version, such as "to #3" | The version that served before the latest change. Shown only when there is one, and off while the service is busy. |
| A version under **History** that's built but not serving | **Roll back** or **Roll forward** at the end of its row | That version. Back when it's older than the live one, forward when it's newer. Off during any of the session's rollouts. |
| A message to the agent | "Roll back harbor." | The previous version, the same as the menu item. |

**History** lists every version Alpha keeps. Click one to open it: it unfolds with its log, and **Details** shows how it was built. A version you can switch to also says it's built and kept but not serving, and which version is.

| State | Meaning | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **live** | Serving every visitor. | Nothing to do. |
| **rolling out** | Being built or switched to now. | Watch it and its **Log**, or [stop it](/publish/publish-your-app#stop-a-rollout) while it builds. |
| **ready** | Built and kept, but not serving. | Roll back or forward to it. |
| **failed** | Never served. Shows the error, and on the newest version a hint. A stopped rollout fails with "the deployment was stopped". | Fix the cause and republish. See [When a publish fails](/publish/monitor#when-a-publish-fails). |
| **gone** (struck through) | No longer exists, because it was removed outside Alpha. | Nothing. It can't be served again. |

<Frame caption="History, newest first. A ready version is one click from serving again, with Roll back or Roll forward at the end of its row.">
  <img src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/suyash-lyzr/alpha-documentation@0bce4e6aa460e3426daece54f29c495f4565999a/images/publish/publish-versions-fold.webp" alt="A History timeline: Version 5 · ready · 2 h ago · by the agent · 2m 38s with Roll forward; Version 4 · live · 14 h ago · from the dialog · 2m 43s with Show log; Version 3 · ready with Roll back; Version 2 · failed · 17 h ago · by the agent · 1m 14s; and Version 1 · ready with Roll back." />
</Frame>

Each switch also posts a card in the transcript, such as "Rolled harbor back to version #3". Your next republish makes the new version live again, so a rollback never holds the app on an old version. Alpha keeps the last [{versionsKept}](/reference/limits), never removing the serving one.

An agent's API gets one more safeguard: a new version must pass a health check to go live, and if it fails on a republish, Alpha puts the previous version back by itself.

## Delete a service

Deleting takes the app offline for good, along with its production database and, if your Alpha gives services an address on its own domain, that address too. The name becomes free for anyone.

Open **⋯** (**More actions**) in the service's header and choose **Delete service**. Its tooltip says whether the production database goes too. **⋯** then gives way to **Delete service** and **Keep** in the header, and there's no other confirmation. The menu item is off while the service is busy.

You can also delete from the service's card on the [**Published** page](/publish/monitor#see-everything-you’ve-published): its trash button asks "Delete harbor and its database?" (or "Delete harbor?" without one), with **Keep** and **Delete**.

<Frame caption="One more click deletes the service. Keep backs out.">
  <img src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/suyash-lyzr/alpha-documentation@0bce4e6aa460e3426daece54f29c495f4565999a/images/publish/publish-delete-confirm.webp" alt="The harbor-bookings header, marked live, where the ⋯ button has given way to a red Delete service button and Keep, beside Edit settings and Republish." />

<Warning>
Deleting a service can't be undone. The app stops answering at once, and its production database is dropped with every row in it. Copy out any production data you need first. Deleting a session deletes all its services and their production databases the same way.
</Warning>

Only you can delete a service: the agent has no way to. Deleting one frees a place toward the limit of {publishPerAccount}. See [Limits](/reference/limits).

## Who can do what

The **Publish** tab belongs to the session's owner. People you [share the session](/sharing) with never see it, admins included, though the transcript's publishing cards show them the address.

| Action | Owner | Viewer | The agent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Republish | Yes | No | Yes, when you ask |
| Stop a rollout | Yes | No | No |
| Change settings | Yes | No | Yes, when you ask |
| Roll back or forward | Yes | No | Yes, when you ask |
| Delete a service | Yes | No | No, never |

Every app you've published is listed on the [**Published** page](/publish/monitor#see-everything-you’ve-published) in the sidebar, where you can open it, go to its session or delete it.
